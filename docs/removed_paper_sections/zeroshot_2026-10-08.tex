\subsubsection{Zero-Shot Category Generalization}
\label{sec:zeroshot}

The substitution above fixes training and perturbs only what happens at
inference: every category was trained normally, and only its query is
swapped afterward. This leaves a different question open. Section~\ref{sec:cross-attn}
builds each category's query from a short text description rather than a
free-standing index precisely so that the category's identity, not just its
position in a fixed list, is available to the model. If that design does
what it suggests, a category's description should let the shared heads
recognize it at test time even for a category the model never had the
chance to learn from directly. We test this
directly rather than assume it: for three categories per domain, the most
frequent, the median, and the rarest by training support (restricted to
categories with at least 15 test occurrences, so the resulting F1 stays
well-defined), we remove that category's positive training signal entirely,
forcing its presence label to absent on every training example while every
other category's labels, and the held-out category's own query text and
dev/test gold labels, are left untouched. This is zero-shot in the sense
that the held-out category contributes no positive training signal, not in
the sense that it is invisible to the model: its query and ACD slot remain
active throughout training, and every one of its training instances
supplies a negative gradient update, so the test is closer to training the
shared head against the category than to simply never training on it. CAGE
is then trained and evaluated as in the main results: 45 runs in total, five
domains times three frequency tiers times three seeds.

The outcome is not a smaller version of the main results; it is a different
regime entirely. Every one of the 15 held-out categories scores
\textbf{exactly 0.00 F1} in every one of its three seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot}), against 64.1 to 94.3 F1 for the same category
trained normally, a gap large enough, and uniform enough, that no
statistical test is needed to see it; we report one anyway (Welch
two-sample, one-sided, $p<0.001$ throughout) for completeness. We checked
that this is a real effect of the model's predictions, not an artifact of how we
score them, by reading the raw predictions directly: for Beauty's
\texttt{Colour}, the gold label is present on 727 of 1,607 test reviews, and
the trained model predicts it on none of them, while every other category
in the same output file is predicted at normal, gold-proportional rates. The
collapse also does not track frequency: zero-shot F1 is 0.00 in every tier, so a
category that would otherwise have had thousands of training examples fails as
completely as one that would have had barely enough to evaluate; the tiers' mean
losses (88.1 F1 for the most frequent, 80.5 for the rarest) differ only because
their supervised F1 does.

This is a qualitatively different failure than substituting a wrong query.
There, the nearest neighbor's query still recovered 7.3 to 24.4 F1, because
the substitute query still pointed at a category the shared heads had
actually learned to detect; even the farthest and the all-zero query
recovered a little (up to 16.3 and 5.7 F1 respectively). Here, the
category's own, correct query recovers nothing, consistent with withholding
its labels not leaving the detector untrained so much as training it toward
the opposite conclusion: every one of the training examples (58 to over 6,000 per held-out
category) where that category's presence label was forced to absent is a gradient
step telling the shared ACD head that whatever pattern this query produces
should never fire, though confirming this from the model's raw presence
scores rather than its thresholded predictions is left to future work. The
query still identifies which category is being asked about, as
Section~\ref{sec:query-swap} shows, but it cannot substitute for positive
training examples of that category.

The zero-shot result of
Section~\ref{sec:zeroshot} sharpens this further: the same description that
recovers 64 to 94 F1 for a normally-trained category recovers nothing for
one whose positive training labels were withheld, so the query's text is not
sufficient on its own; what the evidence credits is specifically the query
together with having been trained on that category.

% ===== [6] Discussion (limitations): removed paragraph, was before "Some baseline cells are single values." =====
\textbf{CAGE, trained as described here, does not recover a category whose positive
training labels are withheld.} Withholding a category's positive training labels entirely,
while leaving its query text and dev/test gold untouched, collapses that
category's F1 to 0.00 in every domain and every seed tested
(Section~\ref{sec:zeroshot}), regardless of how frequent the category would
otherwise have been. The category-text query identifies which category is
being asked about, but it does not let the architecture answer for one whose
positive training labels were withheld; whether loss-masking the category instead of
forcing it to absent, or a per-category rather than shared decision
threshold, would change this outcome is untested.

% ===== [7] Conclusion: "which three further analyses" became "two"; removed passage after the query-substitution sentence =====
A leave-one-category-out zero-shot test
(Section~\ref{sec:zeroshot}) shows what that substitution alone could not: the
query's text is not a substitute for positive training examples of the category either,
since withholding one category's positive training labels, while leaving its
query and test-time gold untouched, drives that category's F1 to 0.00 in all 15
categories tested, across five domains and three frequency tiers, against
64.1 to 94.3 F1 for the same category trained normally. CAGE's category
query tells the shared heads which category to look for; under the training
procedure used throughout this paper, whether they can find it depends on
having been trained to, not on what the query says.

% ===== [8] Conclusion: "Four directions" became "Three"; removed fourth direction =====
Fourth, since Section~\ref{sec:zeroshot} shows CAGE, trained this way,
cannot recognize a category whose positive training labels were withheld, at least
with the one description per category we tested, covering a new category in a live taxonomy today means relabeling and
retraining; closing that gap,
through few-shot adaptation with a handful of labeled examples per new category,
through meta-learning across the category set, or by regularizing the ACD head so
that withheld categories keep some residual sensitivity rather than being actively
suppressed, is a concrete target for future architectures in this space, not just a
caveat on this one.
